What this is & how it works

The FSD-X ORB Auto-Trader is an automated opening range breakout system for futures. It watches the open, takes graded breakout setups, and manages a stop and target for each trade — hands-free.

Three pieces working together
1 · The FSD-X strategy
Runs on your TradingView chart. It decides when to enter, at what stop and target, when to exit, and when to flatten at the end of the day.
2 · PickMyTrade (PMT)
The bridge. It catches the strategy’s signals and routes orders to your broker.
3 · Tradovate
Your broker, where the trade actually executes.

The flow is always Strategy → PickMyTrade → Tradovate. Each link has to be set up correctly and the order of the steps matters, so work through this guide in sequence.

How the strategy talks to PickMyTrade

Your TradingView alert is set to Order fills only. Every time the strategy fills an order — an entry, a stop, a target, or the end-of-day close — TradingView sends PickMyTrade a message containing your current position state.

PickMyTrade reads that state and makes your broker account match it. Go long, it opens a long. Go flat, it closes the position and cancels the resting bracket. This is why the strategy can flatten you at the end of the day without any extra configuration.

Before you begin

This guide assumes the FSD-X strategy is already on your TradingView chart — getting access to the strategy is handled separately. Before setting up automation you need:

A TradingView paid plan
Essential or higher. Automation uses webhook alerts, which the free plan does not support.
A PickMyTrade account
Connected to Tradovate.
A Tradovate sim / demo account
For setup and testing. Do not use a live or funded account until Section 6 is complete.
The strategy on your chart
On the correct symbol and timeframe you were provided.
“Automated” means the clicking is automated
It does not mean you can walk away and ignore it. You still check on it daily. Section 7 covers what to look at, and it matters.
2.1   Set up your chart to match the backtest

The FSD-X backtest was run with the chart configured a specific way. Your live chart must match, or your prices and opening range will not line up with what was tested. Both settings are in the bottom-right toolbar.

B-ADJ (back-adjustment) → OFF
Back-adjustment shifts historical prices to smooth contract rolls. An ORB keys off real traded prices, so leave it off — the button should look flat, not highlighted.
Session → ETH (Electronic Trading Hours)
The backtest measured the opening range on ETH data, not RTH.
If your levels do not match other members
A mismatched B-ADJ or session setting is almost always the reason. An ORB is built entirely on exact prices and the session’s opening range — B-ADJ on shifts your history, and RTH anchors your range to a different time. Either one pulls your live behaviour away from the tested behaviour.
2.2   Use the continuous contract — MNQ1!

Load the continuous front-month symbol, the one ending in 1! — for example MNQ1!. Do not use a dated contract such as MNQU6, and not the plain index symbol.

Why continuous
It stitches every contract together, so your chart carries full price history. The strategy and its backtest need that — a dated contract only has data for its own short lifespan.
It follows the front month
So you never switch charts when contracts roll.
The chart rule is universal; the bridge rule depends on your service
Your chart is always MNQ1!, whatever bridge you use.

PickMyTrade: the TradingView Symbol field must also be MNQ1! — not MNQ, not a dated contract. See Section 4.2.
Other bridges: some accept the continuous symbol, others require the exact front-month contract and need updating at each roll. Check their docs.
Using a different bridge?
This guide is written for PickMyTrade. Others (TradersPost and similar) follow the same flow — Strategy → Bridge → Broker — so the core ideas apply. Check your service’s docs for its alert message format, how it places a bracket, how it maps the contract symbol, and how it reads position state.
3

Strategy settings

Open the settings with the gear icon next to the strategy name on your chart. These are the ones that matter.

3.1   Take Profit Target — TP1 or TP2
TP1
The first, closer target. Hits more often. Recommended starting point.
TP2
Further out. Larger winners, less often. If a setup has no valid TP2 the strategy uses TP1 automatically.

The whole position exits at one target, in full. Start with TP1.

3.2   Exit Handling — Bracket vs Strategy-Managed
Bracket — recommended
Your stop and target are sent with the entry, so PickMyTrade rests them at the broker. They are protected even if your internet drops or a signal is missed, because the orders are already parked and waiting. This guide assumes Bracket.
Strategy-Managed — fallback only
No resting orders. The strategy watches the market and sends a close when your stop or target is reached. If your connection hiccups at the wrong moment that exit may not get through. Use only if your broker cannot support bracket orders.
3.3   The EOD / session close settings

On the MNQ NY Session preset these are locked to tested values and your boxes are ignored — every member flattens at the same time as the backtest. You do not need to change anything.

3.4   Reading the settings panel — what the preset locks

Most inputs are locked by the Instrument Preset. That is deliberate: it keeps every member on the same tested numbers, so your levels, grades and exits match the room and the backtest. Locked inputs are labelled so you can see the active value without guessing:

Flatten By (HHMM CT)  ·  MNQ: 1530  ·  Custom only
MNQ: 1530
The value actually running while the preset is MNQ NY Session.
Custom only
The box is editable only if you switch the preset to Custom (Advanced).
A locked box will still accept your typing — and still ignore it
TradingView cannot grey out or hide an input, so a locked box looks editable. Type 1545 into Flatten By on the MNQ preset and the box will show 1545, but the strategy still flattens at 15:30.

If a change appears to do nothing, check the label. If it reads Custom only, the preset is in charge and the MNQ: value is what is running.

Do not switch the preset to Custom (Advanced) to change one setting
Custom mode unlocks every Custom-only input at once, using whatever values happen to be sitting in your boxes. That changes your stops, grades and entry filters all at the same time and desyncs you from the room.

Custom (Advanced) is for running the strategy on a different instrument, and it needs verifying in the Strategy Tester first. Leave the preset on MNQ NY Session.
3.5   What the strategy trades
Session
Roughly 8:30 AM to 3:30 PM Central. New entries stop earlier in the afternoon; positions are flat by day’s end.
Real breakouts only
It waits for a candle to close beyond the level before entering, which filters out false breakouts, and skips setups where the opening range is too small.
Quality-based sizing
Stronger setups get more contracts, weaker ones fewer. Varying contract counts between trades is normal and by design.

Connecting to PickMyTrade

This is where most setup issues happen. The goal: PickMyTrade places your stop and target as a bracket at Tradovate on every entry.

4.1   Confirm the strategy is exposing your levels

The strategy publishes two values for PMT to read: SL_value (your stop) and TP_value (your target). Verify they exist before touching PMT.

1
Open the Data Window — the icon on the right-hand toolbar, or press the ~ key.
2
Scroll to your strategy’s section. You should see SL_value and TP_value listed.
3
Out of a trade they read empty (n/a) — correct. In a trade they show live prices.

If those names do not appear, the strategy is not loaded correctly. Reload it before continuing.

4.2   Set the TradingView Symbol — MNQ1!

In PMT’s alert builder find the TradingView Symbol field. Type mnq and select MNQ1! from the dropdown. Not plain MNQ, not MNQU6.

This must match your chart. The alerts come from the MNQ1! chart, so PMT needs to listen for MNQ1!. PMT then routes to the current front-month contract at Tradovate, and because the continuous symbol tracks the front month you never rebuild the alert at a roll.

4.3   Set the Stop Loss & Take Profit in PickMyTrade

Set Stop Loss & Take Profit Mode to “Price (From TradingView).” Two boxes appear. Enter these exactly:

Stop Loss box:   {{plot("SL_value")}}
Take Profit box:   {{plot("TP_value")}}
The most common mistake: extra braces
Each entry ends in exactly two closing braces. Never three.

Correct:  {{plot("SL_value")}}
Wrong:    {{plot("SL_value")}}}  ← extra brace; it fails silently.

Two things cause it: the box auto-adds a brace as you type, or you pasted on top of existing text. Clear the box completely (select all, delete) before entering the value, then confirm the end reads close-quote, close-paren, two braces, nothing more.

Confirm your account and token fields are correct — PMT fills these for you — then let PMT generate the alert JSON and copy it.

4.4   Create the alert in TradingView
1
On the chart, click the Alerts icon (alarm clock) → Create Alert.
2
Condition: select the FSD-X strategy, with the trigger set to Order fills only.
3
Message: paste the JSON that PickMyTrade generated.
4
Notifications → Webhook URL: enable it and paste PickMyTrade’s webhook URL.
5
Click Create.
Order fills only is required — do not change it
This is the setting that lets the strategy manage your position, including the end-of-day flatten. Every strategy fill sends PMT your current position state. Change it and entries, exits and the EOD close all stop reaching your broker.
The “line 9” warning is normal
After pasting PMT’s JSON, TradingView may show a red warning such as “Expected property name… at line 9.” This is cosmetic — TradingView validates the raw template before live values are filled in. When the alert fires the placeholder becomes a real price, which is valid.

If TradingView lets you save, save it, then confirm on demo (Section 6). The live result is what matters.
5

End of day: how your position gets closed

The strategy closes your position. PickMyTrade’s Close All is now your backstop.
Previously your end-of-day flatten was handled entirely by PMT’s Close All. The auto-trader now does it directly.
5.1   What actually happens at 15:30 CT
1
At 15:30 Central the strategy closes any open position.
2
That produces an order fill, and your alert is set to Order fills only — so TradingView sends PickMyTrade a message.
3
The message reports your position as flat.
4
PickMyTrade makes your broker account match: it closes the position at Tradovate and cancels the resting bracket orders.

This happens on its own. It needs no configuration beyond the alert you already built in Section 4.

5.2   Half-days are handled too

On listed half-days, CME equity-index futures close around 12:00–12:15 CT and never print a 15:30 bar. The strategy carries a built-in CME date list and flattens at 11:50 CT on those days instead.

This is the part a fixed clock in PickMyTrade cannot do
A Close All set to 15:30 CT fires after the market has already closed on a half-day, so it cannot protect you. The auto-trader’s built-in calendar is what covers those days — which is why Bracket mode is now safe on shortened sessions.
5.3   Setting PickMyTrade’s Close All as a backstop

Keep PMT’s Close All configured. It costs nothing and covers the rare case where TradingView drops an alert or a webhook fails to deliver. The only rule: set it later than 15:30 CT. 15:45 CT is a good choice.

In PMT’s Active Trading Time settings, set the To Time and select Close All. PMT uses Eastern Time, so use the “Your time” readout to confirm what you have chosen reads as the Central time you intended.

Your PMT Close AllWhat happensVerdict
Later than 15:30 CTStrategy flattens at 15:30. PMT finds a flat account and does nothing.Correct — this is the intent
Exactly 15:30 CTBoth fire in the same minute. The position still only closes once.Harmless, but no margin
Earlier than 15:30 CTPMT closes you first. You exit earlier than the strategy was tested on.Avoid
Already set up? One setting to move
If your PMT Close All is currently set to 15:30 CT, move it to 15:45 CT so the auto-trader stays clearly primary and PMT is purely a failsafe. Either way the position only closes once, so there is no urgency. Your alert and JSON are unaffected.
6

Testing on demo

Do not point this at a funded account until you have watched it work on sim.

Let a trade fire, or wait for one during a session, then check:

1
In Tradovate you should see the position open, plus a resting stop order and a resting target order.
2
Those two prices should match the SL and TP lines on your chart.
3
Watch a complete cycle — an entry that closes on its stop or target and leaves you flat with no leftover orders.
4
Watch the end-of-day flatten. At 15:30 CT the position should close and the bracket should clear. Check your PMT log for the message that arrived at 15:30 — that is the strategy’s flatten reaching the broker.
If the bracket appears at the correct prices and the 15:30 flatten lands, your setup is working.

Daily use & what to watch

“Automated” is not “unattended”
The system removes the emotion and the manual clicking. It does not remove you. Anyone who sets this up and walks away for a week eventually meets a nasty surprise — a broker disconnect, an odd fill, a stuck order — discovered days later with real money already gone.

Check it once a day. It takes about a minute. You are not watching every tick, just confirming it is alive and behaving.
What to look at in your PickMyTrade log
Entries fired and placed orders
With no rejects.
Correct side and quantity
A long signal became a buy, at the contract count you expected.
Stop and target rested at the right prices
Compare them to the lines on your chart.
A message arrived at 15:30 CT
And left you flat.
No rejects or disconnects
These are what break automation, and they hide in the log.

In your first sessions also watch the 8:30 AM Central open — the busiest, highest-slippage moment of the day. Fills are at their worst there. Normal, but be aware.

8

The rules

Half-days are handled — with one exception

As of v2.0 the auto-trader’s early-close calendar flattens you at 11:50 CT on listed half-days, so you no longer need to switch the bot off for the day after Thanksgiving, Christmas Eve and similar sessions.

The exception: July 2027
The calendar is maintained yearly from CME’s published holiday schedule, and years where July 4th falls on a weekend are the hard ones to pin down in advance. July 2027 currently has no entry.

Sit out the first week of July 2027 unless you have confirmed the calendar has been updated. These are thin, low-quality sessions anyway.
Slippage is normal

Your entries will not land exactly at the price shown on the chart. The strategy waits for a candle to close beyond the level — this is what filters out false breakouts — so by the time you are filled price has moved slightly. That difference is the cost of a higher-quality entry.

Stay on demo until you trust it

Run sim for your first sessions. Move to a funded account only after you have watched the system handle real conditions: entries, a full exit cycle, and an end-of-day flatten.

Keep your credentials private
Your PickMyTrade token and account ID are embedded in your alert setup. Anyone who obtains them could send orders to your account. Never share screenshots of your JSON or exported alert logs — they contain your token. If you ever have, regenerate the token in PickMyTrade immediately.

PMT will not save, or a “line 9” JSON errorUsually cosmetic. If TradingView lets you save, save it and test on demo. If a field really is the issue, check the SL/TP placeholders end in two braces, not three.
SL_value / TP_value missing from the Data WindowThe current strategy is not loaded on the chart. Reload it, save, and re-check.
Chart history looks short, or levels do not match other membersYou are likely on a dated contract instead of MNQ1!. Switch the chart (Section 2.2) and set PMT’s symbol to match. Also re-check B-ADJ off and Session = ETH.
