Dashboard

dashboard.html · Your daily starting point

Open →

The page you land on after logging in. It is a status board, not a tool — a glance tells you what the market is doing, what you have posted lately, and what still needs setting up. Nothing here is edited; everything links out.

What is on it
FSD-X Updates
Announcements from us — new builds, changed rules, anything you need to know before the open. New items are flagged.
Market Sessions
Which sessions are open right now, in each market’s own local time. Useful when a setup depends on London or Asia being live.
Equity Curve
Your journal’s cumulative P&L, drawn from your own logged trades. Empty until you log something.
Economic Calendar
High, medium and low impact events for the day, with a link out to the full calendar. Knightfall has no news filter by design — checking this is your job, not the script’s.
Month at a glance
Monthly P&L, win rate, days traded and trade count, plus a Mon–Fri calendar strip. Straight from your journal.
Quick Access
Jump straight into TradingView, the Nexus extension, the VIP Discord, the Risk Profile Planner or the Backtest Research page.
First time here? Do these five things
1
Claim your indicators on Whop. ORB PRO is invite-only, so it has to be claimed before it appears in your TradingView scripts. You need your TradingView username — not your email. Wrong one is the single most common reason access does not show up.
2
Claim your Discord access on Whop. Same place. Claim it on your membership and authorise Discord. That is what puts you in the live trade room at 8:30 AM CT every market morning.
3
Set a password. You can sign in without one, but you will want it before the next step.
4
Turn on two-factor. An authenticator code on top of your password. Needs a password first — that is why it is step four and not step three.
5
Have a look around. Playbook for today’s rules and session clock, Backtest for the historical sessions, Scout Alerts for live signals.
Dashboard looks empty?

That is expected on day one. The equity curve, monthly stats and calendar all read from your journal, so they fill in once you log or import your first trades. Nothing is broken.

Journal

journal.html · Live trade journal and analytics

Open →

Your real trading record. Everything you actually traded lives here, and every stat on the dashboard is calculated from it. Keep it clean and the rest of the platform tells you the truth.

Journal or Backtest?

The journal is for trades that really happened in a real account — funded, evaluation or personal. Anything simulated, replayed or historical belongs in a Backtest session instead. Mixing the two contaminates your live numbers and untangling it later is a chore.

The four tabs
Calendar
Mon–Fri grid with a weekly column. The fastest way to see the shape of a month — where the good runs and the dead patches sat.
History
Every trade in a table: date, ticker, direction, grade, result, TP1, TP2, net P&L, account and a chart link. Filter by period or set a custom range.
Stats
The full analytics set — cumulative P&L, outcome breakdown, runner stats, commissions and fees, contracts, by grade, long vs short, day of week, time of day, by account, and drawdown metrics.
Settings
Journal defaults, a shortcut to Accounts, and the danger zone.
Getting trades in — three ways
1
Import a CSV. ↑ Import takes you to the Trade Importer. Best for Tradovate order history or anything you already have as a file. Covered in full in the Trade Importer section above.
2
Log one by hand. + Log Trade opens the form. Date, entry and exit time, ticker, account, direction, contracts, prices, grade, and optional tags, chart URL and notes.
3
Let Nexus do it. The Nexus 2.0 Chrome extension can push trades straight in from TradingView.
Logging a trade properly
P&L Entry Mode — By Price vs Manual $
By Price means you enter prices and the platform does the arithmetic. Use it — it catches fat-finger mistakes. Manual $ is for when you only have the dollar figure.
TP1 and TP2
TP1 is your first target: exit price and contracts out. TP2 is the runner and is optional. Fill both when you scale out and the Runner Stats panel becomes meaningful.
Account and account type
Tagging each trade to an account is what makes the By Account breakdown and your rule trackers work. Skip it and those panels stay blank.
Grade
A+ through F. This is what powers the By Grade panel — the most useful filter you have for ORB PRO trades.
# of Accounts
If you copy the same trade across several prop accounts, this multiplies it rather than making you log it five times.
Getting data out
↓ CSV
Exports what you are currently looking at — filters applied. Filter to A+ only and you get an A+ only file.
↓ Card
A shareable summary card for a trade or period.
Settings and defaults

Set a default ticker, timeframe, contract count and strategy and the Log Trade form pre-fills them every time. Leave a field blank to not pre-fill it. Small thing that saves a lot of typing if you trade the same instrument daily.

Danger zone

Clear All Trades permanently deletes every trade in your journal — stats, calendar and history all wiped. It makes you type DELETE to confirm, and there is no undo. To remove just one import or one account’s trades, use Accounts → Imports instead. Export a CSV first if there is any doubt.

Common problems
Stats look wrong after an import. Almost always a commission rate mismatch. Fix it in Accounts → Imports by removing that one batch, then re-import at the right rate.
By Account panel is empty. Trades were logged without an account tagged. Set up accounts first, then tag as you go.
A trade is in the wrong account. Open it from History and hit Edit — you do not need to delete and re-add.
Backtest trades ended up here. Delete them from the journal and re-import to a Backtest session. Accounts → Imports can remove the whole batch in one go.

Accounts

accounts.html · Setup, trackers and undo

Open →

Where you set up the accounts you actually trade, the rules you are held to, and where you undo an import that went in wrong. Three tabs: My Accounts, Trackers, Imports.

1. Adding an account
Nickname
Required. Whatever you will recognise — “Apex 50K #2” beats “Account 3”.
Account #
Optional but do it. This must match the Account column in your Tradovate export. Get it right and imported trades route themselves to the correct account with no work from you — which is the whole point when you are running ten prop accounts off ten separate logins.
Account size
Optional. Fill it in or drawdown percentages have nothing to measure against.
Type
Funded, Evaluation or Personal. Drives the account-type filters everywhere else.
Commission
Optional. An all-in per-side rate — commission plus exchange, NFA and clearing — charged on entry and again on exit. It is not a round-turn figure. Common range $0.37–$0.85.
2. Trackers — the part most people skip

A tracker watches one account and tells you where you stand without you doing arithmetic mid-session. It reads straight from your journal. Two halves, and the difference matters:

Firm Rules
The hard limits your prop firm sets — account size, profit target, firm daily loss limit, max or trailing drawdown, and whether the drawdown is trailing or end-of-day. Breach one of these and the account is gone.
My Rules
Your own limits. Personal daily loss limit, max trades per day, max consecutive losses, minimum acceptable grade, and a trade window with a start and end time. Breaking these does not end your account — it moves your discipline score.
Why bother with My Rules

Firm rules tell you when you are dead. Your own rules tell you when you are drifting — usually days earlier. Setting a max consecutive losses of 3 and a trade window that ends at 10:30 catches the two things that actually blow accounts: revenge trading and afternoon boredom.

You can point a tracker at one specific account, at an account type, or at everything — leave both selectors on “Any” to track your whole journal.

3. Imports — the undo button

Every upload is tracked as its own batch. If one went in at the wrong commission rate or against the wrong account, remove that batch alone and everything else stays untouched. This is the fix for a bad import — not clearing your journal.

Danger zone

Delete Trades by Account removes trades for one account only, optionally inside a date range, and shows you what would go before it goes. Permanent, no undo. Use Imports first if you are trying to reverse a single upload.

Common problems
Imported trades did not route to the right account. The Account # does not match the Account column in your export. Fix the number here, remove the batch in Imports, re-import.
Tracker shows nothing. Its filters do not match any trades. Check the account name and type — set both to “Any” to confirm the tracker itself works.
Drawdown percentages look odd. Account size is blank. Add it and they recalculate.
P&L is off by a consistent amount. Commission rate. Remember it is per side, not round-turn — entering a round-turn figure doubles your costs.

Trade Importer

converter.html · Universal CSV importer

Open →

The importer takes a trade CSV, works out what format it is, and turns it into structured trades the rest of the platform can read. It is not FSD-X specific — any TradingView strategy export works, and so does live order history from Tradovate. If you are testing another system, you can analyze it here too.

What it accepts
FX
FSD-X ORB Pro — a TradingView strategy export of ORB PRO / Knightfall. Pulls the grade (A+ / A / B+ / B / C+ / C), direction and contract count straight out of the signal text. Both the older and the newer TradingView export layouts are handled.
TV
TradingView Strategy — any strategy backtest, exported from the List of Trades tab in the Strategy Tester. Prices, P&L, direction and timestamps map over automatically. The grade column stays blank, since only ORB PRO writes grades into its signals.
TD
Tradovate Live Orders Beta — your real fills. Export from Account → Orders. Entries and exits are paired up for you.
JR
FSD-X Journal export — a CSV you previously exported out of the platform. Already paired, so it comes back in as-is. Useful for moving data between sessions or restoring a backup.

You don't pick the format. It's detected from the file, and the badge at the top of Conversion Options tells you what it found. If that badge says something you didn't expect, stop — you've probably grabbed the wrong export.

Running an import
1
Get your file in. Two ways. Upload File takes a drag-and-drop or a file picker. Paste CSV takes raw text — useful when you're copying out of a spreadsheet. If you paste, include the header row or nothing will be detected.
2
Drop several files at once if you need to. This is built for prop traders. Each prop account is its own login, so a normal export session means five or ten separate CSVs. Drop them all together — each file is detected on its own terms, and trades route to the matching account by account number. Identical trades appearing in more than one file are dropped automatically so you don't double count.
3
Set your Conversion Options. Ticker overrides whatever was auto-detected. Date From and Date To trim the range — leave them alone to take everything. They pre-fill from the file, so most of the time you don't touch them.

Commission per side only appears for Tradovate imports, because that's the only case where commissions aren't already in the file. It's an all-in rate — commission plus exchange, NFA and clearing — deducted per contract on both entry and exit, so your imported P&L lines up with Tradovate's Total P/L. Common range is $0.37–$0.85. Set it to 0 if you want gross.

Hit Convert →.
4
Check the preview before you commit. You get a cumulative P&L curve and the first ten trades — date, entry and exit time, ticker, direction, grade, result, net P&L, contracts. Thirty seconds here saves you cleaning up a bad import later. Two things worth eyeballing: does the trade count look right, and did the grades come through.
5
Choose where it goes. This is the decision that matters most.
📓 Journal
Your real trading history. Feeds your main stats, your dashboard and your account tracking. Live fills belong here.
🧪 Backtest
A walled-off session with its own stats. Nothing touches your journal. Simulated or historical data belongs here.
Rule of thumb: if the trades were never actually filled in a real account, send them to Backtest. Mixing backtest data into your journal contaminates your real numbers, and untangling it afterwards is a chore.
6
Fill in the destination details.

Going to Journal: pick an account and an account type (Funded / Evaluation / Personal). The account picker is a fallback only — trades that already matched a saved account number routed themselves and won't be overwritten. Account type auto-fills once you choose an account.

Going to Backtest: either merge into an existing session, or create a new one. A new session needs a name; ticker, strategy and start date are optional but make the session list far easier to read six months from now.
7
Handle duplicates if it asks. If some of these trades are already saved, you get three choices: Skip Duplicates imports only what's new — the safe default for a routine top-up. Replace Existing overwrites the saved copies, which is what you want when you're re-importing at a corrected commission rate. Import Everything Anyway keeps both copies — rarely what you want.
↓ Download CSV

Instead of importing, you can download the converted file. It's the cleaned, paired, standardized version of whatever you fed in. Handy for sharing a set of trades in Discord, keeping an offline backup, or importing manually later.

// Try it — the 7-year backtest

The fastest way to learn the importer is to run real data through it. The full ORB PRO backtest trade log is on the Backtest Research page — download it there, then bring it back here and load it up. Check the strategy's history yourself instead of taking a summary for it.

Go to Backtest Research →
Drop it in — you should see the green FSD-X ORB Pro (New Format) ✓ badge.
Dates fill themselves in. There's no commission field on this one — the published backtest is gross of commissions, fees and slippage, and the file carries commissions at zero, so your numbers match the site exactly.
Hit Convert, choose Backtest, then Create New Session. Name it something like "ORB PRO 7 Year", ticker MNQ, strategy ORB PRO.
Then head to the Backtest page and start slicing it. Section below covers that.
Common problems
Nothing detected on a paste. The header row is missing. Copy it again including the column names.
Grades came through blank on an ORB PRO file. You exported a summary tab rather than List of Trades, or the strategy's signal text was edited. Grades are read out of the signal.
Imported P&L doesn't match Tradovate. Commission rate. Re-import at the corrected rate and pick Replace Existing so you don't end up with two copies.
Backtest trades ended up in your journal. Wrong destination at step 5. Delete them out of the journal and re-import to a Backtest session.
Only some trades imported. Check Date From / Date To — they may have been narrowed on a previous run.

Backtest

backtest.html · Sessions and performance analytics

Open →

Backtest is where trade data goes to be examined. It's the same analytics engine as your journal, pointed at data that isn't your live record. Every session is sealed off — its own trades, its own stats, its own equity curve — so you can hold ten strategies side by side without them bleeding into each other.

Two views, don't mix them up
The main view
Everything across all your logged trades, with Calendar, History, Stats and Backtest tabs. Broad look at the whole picture.
Backtest Sessions
One strategy, one dataset, isolated. Open a session and every stat on screen describes that session only. This is where the real work happens.
Creating a session
1
Three ways in: hit + New Session on the Backtest page, create one during an import from the Trade Importer, or create one from the Nexus extension.
2
Fill in Session Name (required), Ticker, Account Size, Strategy and Start Date. Only the name is mandatory, but account size is what makes drawdown numbers mean anything, so fill it in.
3
Get trades into it — import a CSV through the Trade Importer, use ↑ Import inside the session, or log trades by hand with + Log Trade.

Name sessions so you'll recognize them later. "ORB PRO 7 Year — default settings" beats "test 3". When you're comparing two variants of the same strategy, put the difference in the name.

Filters — the actual point

Headline numbers tell you almost nothing. Filters are how you find out where performance actually comes from. They stack, and ✕ Clear resets everything.

›
Result — Win / Loss / BE. Isolate the losers and look for what they have in common.
›
Account type — Funded / Evaluation / Personal.
›
Grade — A+ through D. The single most useful filter on ORB PRO data. Pull A+ only, note the numbers, then pull C only and compare. That gap is the filtering doing its job.
›
Direction — Long / Short. Tells you whether the edge is symmetrical or leaning one way.
›
Date range — Today, This Week, This Month, All Time, or Custom. Custom is how you go year by year.
Inside a session
Trades
Every trade in the session — date, ticker, direction, grade, result, net P&L, tags, and a chart link where one exists.
Stats
The full analytics set. Breakdown below.
Calendar
Mon–Fri grid with a weekly column, so you see the shape of a month rather than a number. Where losing streaks and dead patches become obvious. Walk back through years of months here.

↓ CSV exports the session — filters applied. Export an A+-only view and you get an A+-only file. ↑ Import adds more trades to the session you're already in.

What every stat panel shows
Cumulative P&L — the equity curve. Shape matters more than the endpoint: a steady climb and a spiky one that finishes level are very different systems.
Outcome Breakdown — wins, losses, breakevens as proportions.
Runner Stats — how the TP2 runner portion performed. Whether letting it run is paying for itself.
Trade Summary — counts, averages, the headline aggregates.
Commissions & Fees — total cost drag. On a high-frequency system this is bigger than people expect.
Contracts — position sizing distribution across the sample.
By Grade — performance split A+ through D. On ORB PRO data, start here.
Long vs Short — directional split.
Day of Week — which sessions carry the results and which drag.
Time of Day — entry-time distribution and how each window performed.
Drawdown Metrics and Drawdown From Peak — worst-case depth and how long recovery took. On an eval account this decides whether a strategy is survivable, regardless of what the net number says. Don't skip it.
By Account appears in the main view rather than inside a session, since a session is a single dataset.
Logging a trade by hand

For replay work, or a setup you took that no export will ever contain. + Log Trade opens the form.

Fill in date, entry and exit time, ticker, and direction. Ticker sets the tick value — MNQ is $0.50 a tick — or you can enter a custom $/point.

P&L Entry Mode is the choice to make. By Price means you enter prices and the platform does the math — use this, it catches mistakes. Manual $ means you type the dollar figure yourself, for when you only have the number.

Enter total contracts, entry price, stop price, and commissions. Then TP1 — exit price and contracts out. TP2 is the runner and is optional. P&L calculates as you go, and the result classifies itself from the total.

Editing and deleting sessions

Edit Session changes the name, ticker, account size, strategy or start date — the trades stay put. Delete Session & All Trades does exactly what it says, and there's no undo. Export to CSV first if there's any chance you'll want it back.

Playbook

playbook.html · Rules, setups and risk profiles

Open →

The plan, kept in one place and synced with Nexus. Everything else on the site records what you did; the Playbook is where you write down what you intend to do. Six tabs.

Today

Your pre-session view. Shows the pre-session checklist, your risk per trade, the session window, and the rules you have flagged for this session. The checklist resets automatically at the open, so it is a fresh set of ticks every morning.

The rest of the tabs
Rules
Rules you hold yourself to, sorted into stages: before the bell, sizing and risk, when it goes wrong, when it goes right, end of day. The stages matter — a rule you need at 8:29 is no use buried in a list of thirty.
Checklist
The items that appear on Today before every session. Keep it short enough that you will actually do it.
Setups
Named setups with their conditions, one per line. Useful for anything you trade alongside ORB PRO.
Goals
Weekly and monthly targets.
Risk Profiles
The eval and daily-driver planner — account size, base risk, backtest window and daily loss limit. Same data as the Eval Profile tab on the Backtest Research page, kept here so it sits next to your own journal rather than on a public page.
Auto-save

Changes save as you type — you will see “Saving…” then “✓ Saved” in the header. There is no save button to forget.

Start with three rules, not thirty

A rule list nobody reads is decoration. Pick the three that would have stopped your worst three trades of the last month, put them in the right stages, and add more only when those three are automatic.

Alerts

alerts.html · Scout Alerts feed and settings

Open →

The same alerts Scout posts to the VIP Discord, shown on the site. Two tabs: Feed for the live stream, Settings for what interrupts you.

Settings
Pop-up alerts
Shows alerts in the corner of the screen while you are on the site. Turn it off and nothing pops up anywhere.
Sound
A short chime when an alert appears.
Minimum grade
Only pop graded setups at this grade or better. Note that Setup Brewing, VWAP and the session summary carry no grade, so this does not touch them — use the individual toggles for those.
Which alerts
Per-type toggles. This only controls what pops up on the site; everything still lands in Discord regardless.
Fire a test alert
Sends a sample using your current settings so you can see exactly what will appear before the open.
Settings are per device

These are saved on the device you set them on. Muting alerts on your phone does not mute them on your desktop — which is usually what you want, since you are normally at a desk when you are trading.

Turn the grade filter up before you turn alerts off

If alerts feel noisy, raising the minimum grade to A+ / A usually fixes it. Members who switch pop-ups off entirely tend to forget they did and then wonder why the site is quiet.
